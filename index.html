About

I'm a PhD candidate in Epidemiology at McGill University, supervised by Professor Erin Strumpf. My research explores inequalities in health and access to preventive care, particularly how access can be shaped by where people were born and where they live in Canada.

Before starting my PhD, I worked in applied epidemiology and program evaluation. At Niagara Region Public Health, I led the COVID-19 epidemiology portfolio, led the design of the region's community health surveys, and designed the evaluation of mass immunization clinics. At the Ontario HIV Treatment Network, I evaluated community-based HIV programs as well as supported community partners develop grant applications. I've also worked at the Public Health Agency of Canada and as a statistician at McMaster University's School of Nursing.

My doctoral research is supported by the Fonds de recherche du Québec and a Heather Munroe-Blum Studentship for Social Epidemiology (McGill University). I serve as the student representative on the board of the Canadian Society for Epidemiology and Biostatistics and contribute to its equity, diversity and inclusion working group. I hold an MPH and an MSc in Global Health from McMaster University.
