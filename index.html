Epidemiologist studying inequalities in health and access to care.

I'm Japteg Singh, based in Montreal. I use population data to find where access to care breaks down, and I work with the people who can do something about it.

Work

Cancer screening and immigration

Immigrants to Canada are screened for breast, cervical and colorectal cancer less often than people born here. My doctoral research at McGill asks how large those gaps are and what drives them, so that screening programs know where to act.

The public health workforce

Public health depends on its nurses, and many are stretched thin. With colleagues at McMaster I track how that workforce has changed over the past decade and what burnout is doing to its capacity.

About

I'm a social epidemiologist. I'm interested in how the circumstances of people's lives, such as where they were born and where they live, shape their health and the care they receive.

I'm completing a PhD in epidemiology at McGill University, funded by the Fonds de recherche du Québec. Before that I was an epidemiologist at Niagara Region Public Health.

I serve as a student board member of the Canadian Society for Epidemiology and Biostatistics, where I work on making the field more equitable, and I previously sat on the board of the Canadian Public Health Association.
